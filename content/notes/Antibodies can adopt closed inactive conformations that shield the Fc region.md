#### Summary

**Some antibodies can adopt closed, inactive conformations in which the [[Fab]] arms fold over the Fc region, shielding sites involved in effector functions** [@chrone2026]. In this model, inactivity concerns Fc-mediated effector activity; the antigen-binding sites can remain exposed.

#### Details

The familiar open Y shape represents only part of the antibody conformational ensemble. Closed states need not be a single rigid structure, and their prevalence can vary among antibodies and experimental conditions. These observations should not be generalized to all antibodies or even every member of an IgG subclass.

Opening could expose Fc-associated sites and permit effector activation. Antigen binding has been proposed to trigger this transition, but direct evidence for that mechanism remains distinct from evidence for a closed state.

Natalizumab provides one example: cross-linking mass spectrometry, modeling, and solution assays support a compact IgG4 arrangement. Heating increased accessibility in these assays; low-pH results were less conclusive because altered cross-linking efficiency could contribute [@chrone2026].
