#### Summary
**Raw sequence abundance after an [[Next-generation sequencing|NGS]]-based selection is not a quantitative measure of binding affinity** [@paul2026; @makowski2022]. Quantitative sequencing assays can nevertheless recover affinity: Tite-Seq estimates $K_d$ from sequence distributions across antigen titrations and fluorescence-activated sorting bins [@adams2016]. In a blinded HCDR3-cluster benchmark, most computational affinity rankings also underperformed random clone selection, with the most abundant clone serving as a stronger baseline than most submissions [@erasmus2026].
